# Write a connected map

Read for genre `map`: field exploration, repository orientation, origins/consumers or bounded change impact. A map teaches distinctions and relationships; it is not an inventory of files, vendors or search results.

## Find the organizing question

State the reader's question and examined boundary, then the distinction that makes the territory navigable. For a field, distinguish mechanisms by the problem they solve and conditions they assume. For code, distinguish entry, state owner, publication/consumer and selection. For impact, start from the exact delta rather than the repository's folder tree.

Explain why the chosen categories belong together and where they overlap. Revise categories when evidence crosses them; do not force a false exclusive taxonomy. A map may show several plausible paths without ranking them.

## Connect nodes with reasons

For each material node give its role, relevant evidence and boundary. Explain each important edge: calls, registers, publishes, consumes, generates, persists or conditionally depends on. An event edge is not a synchronous call; a lexical candidate is not resolved identity; an unobserved bootstrap remains unobserved.

Use a diagram only if its labels/legend retain these distinctions and its edges have source witnesses. Pair it with prose explaining the relationship that answers the question. In a field map, connect representative techniques to needs, assumptions and counterexamples rather than drawing an unexplained constellation.

For an impact map, say what a consumer relies on and why the delta could affect it. Differentiate required regeneration, possible behavior change and unknown exposure. A broad list with no consequences cannot guide integration.

## Preserve the frontier

Describe important excluded branches, unsupported semantic coverage and inaccessible material. “Examined sources show these families” is different from “these are all approaches”. The absence of a candidate in a top-K search does not establish a gap. Known positive controls support only their declared detector scope.

Contrary examples that change categories or reveal a protected path belong next to the affected node. Preserve them in the corpus rather than hiding them behind a clean diagram. Cross-links explain a relation; they do not make evidence independent.

## End with productive navigation

Tell the reader where to start and which next question the map makes worth asking. Justify each promising branch by the evidence or unresolved distinction. Do not end with a generic backlog or invent product priority from a research category.

Check actual argument order using [synthesis](synthesis.md). Publish the map through native [document recording](../../jimmy-discovery/references/record-tools.md) when promised, with actual findings/citations. Close when the map supports useful navigation within its explicit frontier, or report the missing seam and next discriminator using [closure](../../jimmy-discovery/references/closure.md).
