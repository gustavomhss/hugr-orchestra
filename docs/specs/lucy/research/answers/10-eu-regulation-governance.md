## Answer

**Conclusion.** A reviewer of software changes is bound most directly by the EU AI Act, the Cyber Resilience Act (CRA), NIS2 and the revised Product Liability Directive (PLD). The AI Act's high-risk duties cover logging and human oversight. The CRA requires secure design, vulnerability handling for the whole support period and incident reporting. NIS2 makes top management accountable for cybersecurity risk measures. The PLD makes manufacturers liable for software defects, including ones that show up through later updates. On the assurance side, SLSA build provenance and the NIST SSDF can be turned into machine checks. Tools such as OPA, Kyverno, ORT and axe-core can enforce parts of this as policy-as-code. An AI reviewer can gather and sign evidence, but under the AI Act's human-oversight design the person accountable must stay able to override it, so the final compliance verdict stays with a person.

**Claims**
- read: AI Act application dates (Commission page). GPAI obligations applied from 2 August 2025. Transparency rules apply from August 2026. High-risk systems in Annex III areas apply from 2 December 2027, and high-risk systems built into products (Annex I) from 2 August 2028, after the AI Omnibus.
- read: AI Act Article 12 says high-risk systems "shall technically allow for the automatic recording of events (logs) over the lifetime of the system".
- read: AI Act Article 14(4) says the people doing oversight must stay aware of automation bias and be able to "disregard, override or reverse the output".
- read: CRA (Commission summary). In force since 10 December 2024. The Article 14 reporting obligations apply from 11 September 2026, and the regulation applies in full from 11 December 2027. Annex I Part II requires vulnerabilities, including those in components, to be "handled effectively" for the support period. Products placed on the market before 11 December 2027 fall under the CRA only after a substantial modification.
- read: NIS2 makes top management accountable for non-compliance with cybersecurity risk-management measures. The transposition deadline was 17 October 2024.
- read: the revised PLD covers all software, including AI systems. A manufacturer is liable for defects present at release, including ones that appear later through updates or machine learning. It applies to products placed on the market from 9 December 2026.
- read: the Commission's European Accessibility Act page says Member States had to transpose the Act by June 2022.
- read: SLSA Build L2 requires signed provenance generated by a hosted build platform. Build L3 requires that signing secrets are not reachable from user-defined build steps. Note that the captured v1.0 page says v1.2 is now the current version.
- read: NIST SP 800-218 (SSDF v1.1) was published in February 2022.
- read: licences as reported by GitHub. OPA, Kyverno and ORT are Apache-2.0, and axe-core is MPL-2.0. For ScanCode and Conftest GitHub reports NOASSERTION, so their licences were not determined here.
- inferred: mapping to checks.
  - Automatic, on the diff or release: SBOM present and refreshed per release, dependency CVE scan, licence scan with ORT or ScanCode, SLSA provenance signature check, logging hooks present for high-risk AI features, axe-core run on UI changes.
  - Human or legal judgement: AI risk class, whether a change is a CRA "substantial modification", whether a defect could create PLD exposure, oversight design, and EAA scope.
- inferred: evidence an auditor will accept. Use append-only, signed records tied to the commit and build digest, in the style of SLSA provenance. Separate the author, the reviewer and the approver. Record a named human sign-off. The AI produces findings, not verdicts.

**Not examined.** EUR-Lex returned empty pages and the Commission's DORA page failed to load, so I could not capture:
- the Official Journal texts;
- DORA and its RTS 2024/1774 Article 17 on ICT change management;
- the CRA SBOM wording and the 24-hour reporting deadline;
- EN 301 549/WCAG, the Data Act and the DSA.

I also did not capture ISO/IEC 27002, 27701 or 42001 control text (paywalled, and ISO's site restricts AI use of its content), SOC 2 CC8.1, the NIST AI RMF, the OpenSSF Scorecard, REUSE/SPDX, or the SSDF practice tables. Those claims are left out rather than asserted. This answer is not legal advice.
