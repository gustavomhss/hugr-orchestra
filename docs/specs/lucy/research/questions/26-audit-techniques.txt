Audit techniques from the assurance professions applied to software and engineering processes: what methods do internal, IT and external auditors use, and how can an AI auditor apply them to a software delivery process and its evidence? Use primary sources (IIA Global Internal Audit Standards, ISACA ITAF and COBIT, AICPA SOC 2 and attestation standards on evidence and sampling, ISO 19011 audit guidelines, PCAOB/IAASB on audit evidence and IT general controls, continuous auditing literature, digital forensics standards).
Subquestions: (1) the audit process and techniques: risk-based planning, walkthroughs, inquiry, observation, inspection, re-performance, analytical procedures, tests of design vs operating effectiveness, substantive testing; (2) sampling: statistical and judgmental sampling, sample sizes, how auditors choose which changes, PRs or deployments to test, and full-population testing with data analytics; (3) IT general controls for change management and access: segregation of duties, approval evidence, emergency changes, privileged access, and typical deficiencies; (4) evidence quality: sufficiency and appropriateness, reliability hierarchy, chain of custody, tamper evidence, audit trail completeness, professional skepticism, and forensic techniques for detecting manipulated evidence or history; (5) continuous auditing and continuous control monitoring, and how an AI process auditor (Orchestra's `frankie` seat or a lucy audit mode) should design its checks, findings classification (deficiency, significant deficiency, material weakness) and reports.
